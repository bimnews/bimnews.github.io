v0829	A photographic and narrative volume born from a walk along the Regio Tratturo Pescasseroli–Candela: 211 kilometres across Abruzzo, Molise and Apulia, on the route of transhumance. The images tell of landscapes, rural architecture and the religious and secular traditions of pastoral civilisation. The book accompanies the touring exhibition presented in 2017 in Bari and Foggia, with an introduction by Giorgio Otranto and a preface by Archbishop Francesco Cacucci.
v0830	First of all because my father bought it for me, which he doesn't usually do. To tell the truth I nudged him a little: we had gone together to the book's presentation, given by one of my university professors, the architect <a href="https://www.ansa.it/puglia/notizie/2021/08/19/morto-arturo-cucciollasindaco-bari-perso-grande-intellettuale_49005c55-d327-4eb8-8ce0-793c9d2defd8.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Arturo Cucciolla</a> of the Politecnico di Bari, who died in 2021. That is another reason why I like to remember this book.
v0831	It ties me to my homeland and also reminds me of broken promises. It is not the book that tells this, but the meeting I attended: my professor was trying to revive these immense motorways of the past, the tratturi, along which millions of head of livestock (millions, not thousands), mostly sheep, moved every year between the summer pastures of Abruzzo and the plains of Apulia. A recovery that could have been, and was not.
v0832	The book is also precious for its photographs of the architecture of transhumance: the stone enclosures that protected the flocks from wolves, or the so-called contapecore, an arch under which the sheep passed to be counted and directed towards the farmsteads, where the shepherds stopped to rest. It is the story of another world, which in my opinion deserves to be explored further.
v0833	The Photographer's Eye
v0834	Composition and Design for Better Digital Photos · Michael Freeman · Italian edition Logos
v0835	The composition manual by the British photographer and author Michael Freeman, published in English in 2007 as <i>The Photographer's Eye</i>. It explains how an image is built: the frame, the graphic elements (points, lines, shapes, rhythm), light and colour, up to the photographer's intention. The rules are not recipes to apply, but tools to understand, also so as to know when to break them.
v0836	It takes me back to the photography courses I attended at Domenico Fornarelli's studio in Bari, Studio Fotografico Fornarelli, which has now become <a href="https://domenicofornarelli.wixsite.com/evoluxfotografia/about" target="_blank" rel="noopener">EVOLUX – Centro Polifunzionale di Fotografia</a>. There I learned to look before shooting, which is exactly the theme of this book.
v0837	With Domenico I also shared the years of <a href="https://www.facebook.com/aggregarti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">AggregARTI</a>, the cultural association he chaired: a group born around photography and open to every form of culture. I fondly remember the good times and the events we experienced together. The association closed too soon, but that experience stayed with me, and every time I leaf through this book it comes back with him.
v0838	Domenico Fornarelli continues to teach and curate exhibitions: these days in Bari there is <a href="https://www.puglialive.net/bari-visioni-una-mostra-fotografica-collettiva-che-debuttera-a-bari-dal-6-al-21-novembre-2026-presso-il-teatro-e-cinema-polifunzionale-anchecinema-corso-italia-112/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">«Visioni»</a>, the group show he curated at AncheCinema (6–21 November 2026).
v0839	Economics
v0840	The books that helped me understand how the economy works, beyond the slogans.<span class="acc-count">· 2 entries</span>
v0841	Capire l'economia For Dummies
v0842	Roberto Fini · Hoepli
v0843	An introduction to economics for complete beginners, in the For Dummies series: from the basic concepts, such as markets, prices and money, to the big questions such as inflation, growth and public debt, with examples from current affairs. The author, Roberto Fini, teaches Macroeconomics at the University of Verona and has worked in economic education for years.
v0844	I read it a few years ago and it was the beginning of a much deeper understanding of how the economy works. At the time I was still teaching at school and looking for ideas for civic education on these topics: I too was learning many concepts.
v0845	It matters because from there I started reading many other economics books. Before, I had read another kind, more sensationalist, which however did not give me concrete skills: for example <i>The Richest Man in Babylon</i>, which I find rather banal, or the books by Alfio Bardolla, to me a lot of sensationalism and little substance. This one, instead, is an excellent start for anyone who really wants to begin to understand something.
v0846	The Psychology of Money
v0847	Timeless lessons on wealth, greed, and happiness · Morgan Housel · Italian edition Hoepli, 2021
v0848	Morgan Housel, a financial journalist and partner at the Collaborative Fund, starts from a simple idea: with money, behaviour matters more than technical expertise. In some twenty short chapters, each built around a story, he talks about the role of luck and risk, the power of compound interest and time, the value of having a margin of safety, the difference between looking rich and being rich, and how the real dividend of money is the freedom to control one's own time.
v0849	This book was above all a confirmation. Everything I had learned before reading it, and the way I had always managed my finances, found its principles in these pages. Reading it also made me realise how lucky I was to have developed, almost on my own, a certain kind of attention towards money.
v0850	That is why it is an important book for me, and I believe everyone should read it.
