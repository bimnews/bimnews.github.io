v0797	Atomic Habits
v0798	An Easy & Proven Way to Build Good Habits & Break Bad Ones · James Clear · Italian edition De Agostini
v0799	The book by the American writer James Clear, published in 2018, on how habits form and how they change. The key ideas: small improvements, even of 1%, that add up over time like compound interest; systems that matter more than goals; the most lasting change starting from identity («what kind of person do I want to become?») rather than from results. And four practical rules: make a habit obvious, attractive, easy and satisfying, and do the opposite to get rid of bad ones.
v0800	If you really want to change a habit, in my opinion the only thing you can do is read this book. The rest, for me, is almost boring.
v0801	It confirmed many things that reached me in diluted form in the world of training and personal growth. When I distanced myself from it, not without pain and sometimes not in the best way, I realised that many ideas I had been offered did not work. I was not the one who was wrong: they were pre-packaged ideas and, in most cases, without a scientific basis.
v0802	<i>Atomic Habits</i> is not a scientific treatise: it cites research, but it comes mainly from years of self-observation. And that is exactly the point, for me: going back to the <i>temet nosce</i>, «know thyself», which is perhaps my favourite phrase and my personal reference, together with <i>kaizen</i>, continuous improvement in small steps.

v0807	Deep Work
v0808	Rules for Focused Success in a Distracted World · Cal Newport · Italian edition ROI Edizioni, 2020
v0809	Cal Newport, a computer scientist and professor at Georgetown University, distinguishes «deep work», that is, demanding activities carried out in full concentration and without distractions, from «shallow work» made of emails, meetings and notifications. The ability to concentrate deeply, he argues, is becoming ever rarer and therefore ever more valuable. To cultivate it he proposes four rules: work deeply, embrace boredom, quit social media, drain the shallows.
v0810	It is a book that anyone who really wants results should always have. It says things that probably any mother could suggest, but it says them in the most incisive way possible: if you do too many things at once you cannot concentrate, and so you don't reach the result you want. Only in that state of deep concentration, almost of <i>flow</i>, can you really achieve everything.
v0811	The 48 Laws of Power
v0812	Robert Greene, with Joost Elffers · Italian edition Baldini & Castoldi
v0813	Robert Greene's first book, published in 1998: a manual on the dynamics of power built on three thousand years of history. Each of the 48 laws, such as never outshining the master, concealing one's intentions or concentrating one's forces, is told through historical episodes, from the European courts to ancient China, showing both those who respected it and those who broke it and paid the price. It is a controversial book: some read it as a praise of cynicism, others as a tool for recognising manipulation and defending oneself against it.
v0814	It was a long book: it really took me a long time to read. And that is exactly why it matters, because when a book takes me a long time it is because I read it calmly and, as I do with every complex book, I try to apply and observe what it says.
v0815	The 48 Laws were extremely useful to me at a time when I was making very important decisions about my work. I needed to react and solve many problems, to stop living life as a mere spectator and really become its protagonist.
v0816	All of Robert Greene's books are interesting from a historical point of view too: they look at history from another angle, a bit as Alessandro Barbero would. Greene, however, draws principles from it, the emotions linked to those principles, and tells them through anecdotes: so everyone can connect their own story to those episodes and understand what to do in certain situations.
v0817	Nutrition: physics and biology
v0818	Two books to read together · Dario Bressanini and Giulia Enders

v0820	<i>Gut: The Inside Story of Our Body's Most Underrated Organ</i> (Italian edition «L'intestino felice») · Giulia Enders <span>(Sonzogno, 2015; new ed. 2025)</span>
v0821	Dario Bressanini is a chemist at the University of Insubria and one of the most followed science communicators in Italy on food. In <i>La dieta termodinamica</i> («The thermodynamic diet») he approaches body weight with the method of physics: the balance between the energy we take in and the energy we use, the reason why so many diets promise miracles and then fail, and what is really needed to lose weight and keep it off.
v0822	Giulia Enders, a German doctor, with <i>Gut</i> (originally <i>Darm mit Charme</i>, 2014) brought to the general public the organ we talk about least: how digestion works, the role of gut bacteria and the dialogue between gut and brain, told with irony and with drawings by her sister Jill.
v0823	Dario Bressanini is a giant of science communication in Italy: he explains everything about nutrition in a rigorously scientific way, with the eye not of the biologist but of the chemist and physicist. <i>La dieta termodinamica</i> confirmed to me what dieticians and nutritionists say: the perfect diet does not exist; the principles of thermodynamics do. Then, of course, biology comes into play, which is very complex, and together with healthy habits it gives the final result: a healthy body.
v0824	<i>Gut</i> is a few years old now, but it still shows how important it is to know well the processes that take place inside us.
