v0601	Replacing the linked IFC with Dynamo
v0602	How many times have we had to update the architect's IFC linked in Revit, losing hosted sockets and fixtures? Two Dynamo graphs solve the problem in a few steps. First they take a snapshot of the link: position, rotation (crucial with georeferenced models) and hosted elements. Then, after reloading the new IFC, they realign it if needed and reassociate the elements. The guide contains the diagram of the steps to follow and explains how to load the files and how to read the report.

v0656	10 tips for working at your best
v0657	Small rules, drawn from experience, for working better in a studio and on your own. For now there are six; the other four will follow.
v0658	<b>1. Automatic saves.</b> Set them up in every piece of software. If you have never done it, do it now.
v0659	<b>2. Time management and productivity.</b> The two-minute rule, the Eisenhower matrix and the other methods: what I use, what I don't and why.
v0660	<b>3. The template.</b> If you work in BIM, «get it into your f***ing heads» (an invented quote, from a film that doesn't exist): to work well you need templates. Every studio should have one, with the views set up and the families already loaded. Take the time to create it and update it now and then with the studio's know-how. It is vital.
v0661	<b>4. The uninterruptible power supply.</b> How to choose one for your workstation, and by what criteria. Every now and then the power goes out, especially in summer. The goal is not to keep working, but to save your work safely without corrupted files and without losing precious hours.
v0662	<b>5. Overtime is nothing to boast about.</b> It means that something in the organisation of the work did not function. With the difference between employment and self-employment.
v0663	<b>6. The value of reflection.</b> Take some time, as a studio or on your own, to tidy up your files and work on small projects that improve the studio's material: everyone will benefit. And every now and then, question your work and your organisation. «What grows slowly puts down deep roots» (a proverb of uncertain origin).
