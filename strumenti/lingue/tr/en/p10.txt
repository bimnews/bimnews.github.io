v0758	Lise Bourbeau · Italian edition Amrita
v0759	Lise Bourbeau, a Canadian author from Québec, argues that many of our behaviours stem from five emotional wounds experienced in childhood. It is a model born in the world of personal growth, not a theory of clinical psychology.

v0767	Daniel Goleman's emotional intelligence

v0771	Daniel Goleman, an American psychologist and science journalist, brought to the general public an idea that had emerged a few years earlier in academic research: to succeed in life, IQ is not enough; the ability to recognise and manage emotions, one's own and those of others, counts at least as much. In <i>Emotional Intelligence</i> he describes it through self-awareness, self-control, motivation, empathy and social skills. In <i>Working with Emotional Intelligence</i> he takes the same argument into the workplace: what sets apart those who work well with others, those who lead a team and those who can handle pressure.
v0772	Why they matter to me

v0778	White Fang
v0779	Jack London · original title <i>White Fang</i> · many Italian editions
v0780	The novel Jack London published in 1906, set in north-western Canada at the time of the Klondike gold rush. It tells the life of White Fang, a wolf-dog, seen almost always through his eyes: his birth in the wild, the law of the strongest, his passage among humans, first among the natives, then in the hands of a cruel master who makes him fight, and finally the meeting with someone who manages to tame him through trust. It is the reverse of <i>The Call of the Wild</i>, in which a domestic dog instead returns to the wild.

v0782	When I started again I became passionate once more, and I realised it was something to keep up: a good hobby, a healthy habit.
