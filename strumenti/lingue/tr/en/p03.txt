v0214	<strong>BIM</strong> stands for <em>Building Information Modeling</em>. To understand what really changes, it helps to look at how design has always been done.
v0215	Andrea Palladio, Villa Rotonda: plan and section, from <i>I quattro libri dell'architettura</i>, 1570. The Metropolitan Museum of Art, CC0.
v0216	<span>1</span> By hand, for centuries
v0217	For centuries a design was a drawing made by hand: plans, sections and elevations, each drawn separately. Palladio's plate already contains all the ingredients we still use today. This approach stayed the same until the computer arrived: separate views, to be kept consistent by hand.
v0218	To lengthen the wall you move the lines, one by one.
v0219	<span>2</span> CAD: the same lines, on a computer
v0220	CAD brings the drawing board into the computer. You still draw <b>lines</b>, more precise, easy to copy and correct. Each line can be given <b>attributes</b>: the layer, the colour, the line type, perhaps a text saying «brick wall». They are labels placed on the drawing that describe it but do not govern it. To lengthen a wall you have to move its lines, and then correct the section, the elevation and the bill of quantities by hand.

v0222	Height

v0224	<span>3</span> BIM: objects with parameters
v0225	In BIM you no longer draw lines, but <b>three-dimensional objects</b>: a wall, a slab, a window. Each object is governed by <b>parameters</b>: values such as length, height and thickness, linked to one another by formulas. The volume is not redrawn, it is calculated. What we see on screen is no longer a drawing but a representation of the data: plans, sections and schedules all come from the same model.
v0226	Why everything changes
v0227	<b>Information can be extracted and added.</b> Quantities, areas, volumes and schedules come out of the model; and the data needed for calculations, tenders, construction and maintenance goes into it.
v0228	<b>Changes are live.</b> If you raise a slab or widen a room, plan, sections, areas, volumes and schedules update together. In CAD the same work took hours.
v0229	<b>One change applies to all.</b> If you change the material of a wall type, all the walls of that type change, throughout the project.
